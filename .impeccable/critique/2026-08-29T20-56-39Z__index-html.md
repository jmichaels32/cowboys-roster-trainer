## Design Specificity Verdict

The interface feels authored in interaction and content, but intentionally neutral in chrome. The editable practice sentence, package-member rail, roster-card anatomy, and football-specific controls clearly belong to this product. The cool-gray/white/violet shell could fit another learning app, but that restraint matches the approved design system. Specificity is strongest on setup and roster and weakest on the sparse deck picker.

The deterministic CLI scan returned no findings but explicitly ran in degraded regex-only mode. The live browser detector found three: undersized score text, a decorative results eyebrow, and use of Inter. The Inter warning is a project-context false positive because DESIGN.md explicitly owns that choice; the other two are valid latent-state findings. The headless overlay succeeded, but no user-visible browser presentation tool was available.

## Overall Impression

The app's central idea is working. Setup feels quick, personal, and unlike a generic form; player cards are unusually scannable for the amount of data they carry. The largest opportunity is to preserve that same clarity when Refine grows: the new Group capability is useful, but the sheet now behaves like roster administration rather than a lightweight learning tool.

## What's Working

- The editable sentence turns several learning choices into one natural, focused setup interaction.
- Mobile roster cards establish a clear hierarchy across face, identity, physical data, college, and learning state without horizontal overflow.
- The visual system is restrained and consistent: neutral surfaces, one action color, border-led depth, native semantics, and very little filler copy.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Quiz alt text reveals the answer**
   - **Why it matters:** VoiceOver can announce the player's name during “Who is this player?”, invalidating the exercise and score.
   - **Fix:** Make quiz imagery non-spoiling while retaining named alt text in roster/reference contexts; provide an intentional accessible equivalent for visual questions.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

2. **[P1] Core headshots are not truly offline**
   - **Why it matters:** Headshots come from remote NFL URLs, so offline face-learning can collapse into `#` placeholders despite the offline-first product promise.
   - **Fix:** cache or bundle headshots, preserve the last good assets, and expose concise retry/update status.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

3. **[P2] Refine overwhelms the app's progressive-disclosure model**
   - **Why it matters:** Group, Position, Learning, and Sort expose 38 simultaneous pills. Users must scan four decisions and predict their combined result.
   - **Fix:** keep one sheet, collapse completed sections into selected summaries, expand one section at a time, and change Apply to a live `Show N players` action.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

4. **[P2] Important mobile labels and targets are undersized**
   - **Why it matters:** learning chips fall to 8px and Refine pills are 40px tall, weakening status recognition and touch accuracy.
   - **Fix:** raise functional text to 11–12px, targets to at least 44px, remove the decorative results eyebrow, and preserve density through spacing.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan (first-timer):** “Recognition,” “mastery check,” “practice priority,” and “verified” are unexplained. Refine presents 38 choices without a result preview.
- **Sam (accessibility-dependent):** native dialogs, radios, focus styling, and the skip link are good; quiz alt text reveals answers, status text is 8px, and Refine targets miss the 44px minimum.
- **Casey (distracted mobile user):** Start sits in the thumb zone and the app avoids typing where possible; offline headshots fail, current sessions cannot resume, and Refine demands four simultaneous decisions.

## Minor Observations

- No horizontal overflow appeared in inspected 390px states.
- The partially cropped package-member rail successfully signals horizontal swiping.
- The 92-player roster is very long, but visible search, lazy imagery, and compact cards mitigate it.
- The results eyebrow “Session complete” duplicates the heading's job and conflicts with the project's no-decorative-eyebrow rule.
- The deck picker feels inert with one available deck and substantial empty space, though “Coming next” is clear.

## Questions to Consider

- If offline use is foundational, why is the most important learning material network-dependent?
- Could Refine retain one sheet while showing only one expanded section and a live `Show N players` action?
- What does “Verified” mean to a first-time user, and is that understanding needed before they start?
- Should screen-reader learners receive a non-spoiling alternative question rather than an image whose alt text gives away the answer?
